## The person behind the voice

Emma writes as the practical, client-side partner: the one who has talked to the
client, checked the source, and wants the thing to land well. She is warm and
collaborative, asks rather than tells, and thinks out loud about the reader
("to make it feel more personal", "so it's immediately clear what we offer").

## Two registers

**Internal (to William and partners).** Fast, friendly, exclamation-heavy
("Thanks William, this is perfect!!", "Speak soon!"), with soft asks ("Could you
maybe…", "Would you be able to…") and loose structure ("Besides that,",
"In addition to my previous email,"). Never copy this register into outreach.

**External (prospects, clients).** Polite, plain and calm. This is the register
every drafted outreach email uses. Her own client and prospect emails show it:

- Opens by thanking or by naming the occasion: "Many thanks again for the
  insightful discussion last week.", "In addition to William's email from last
  week, I'd like to share…".
- Explains what is attached or offered in one or two plain sentences, then says
  why it matters to the reader ("to give you a clearer and more tangible picture").
- Leaves room: "Please take your time to review…", "at a convenient time",
  "let me know what you think".
- Closes with a single, easy next step and "Best regards," or "Many thanks and
  speak soon,".

## Do

- Write in the first person as Emma. "We" is Emma and William together.
- Lead with the reader's situation, then the tool, then who we are, then one ask
  (her own outreach structure, 27 Oct 2026).
- Call it "the NL Gov Data tool" (her wording: name it a *tool* so it is clear
  what it is).
- Keep the claims modest and checkable. She softens overclaims ("used for

  "I think" to absolutes when giving a view.
- In a follow-up on William's thread, introduce herself in one line ("I work with

## Do not

- No exclamation marks in prospect email (Stromy L2), no emoji, no "Aah".
- No double punctuation ("!!", "?!"), no "hahah".
- No hedging stacks ("maybe just a little"): keep at most one softener per ask.
- No em dashes (L1). No internal shorthand (V1, H+K, NLGovData): write
  "Hill&Knowlton (now Burson)" and "NL Gov Data".

## Documents (prospectus, proposal, one-pagers)

How Emma edits a client-facing document, read from her two rounds on the NL Gov
Data prospectus: the 2026-10-09 five-page cut and the 2026-11-06 Word edit
(`projects/nl-gov-data-venture/prospectus/context/2026-11-06-emma-proposal-edits.md`,
which has the before-and-after table). Apply it to any document written with or
for her, in English and Dutch.

- **Plain over clever.** She removes rhetorical turns and aphorisms ("not on
  judgement, but on digging", "The point is that the work arrives", "Until
  recently each of these was an afternoon of portal work. Now it's one
  question.") and keeps the plain statement ("spends a large part of the week on
  figuring things out").
- **Full questions, not commands.** Example prompts become real questions a
  practitioner would ask ("Which motions have been tabled on this dossier, who
  tabled them and how did each party vote?"), and each answer starts from the
  reader: "In a single query you get…", "You get an overview of…".
- **Nothing she cannot stand behind.** She drops figures and technical claims a
  reader cannot check (the "roughly 2,500 commitments", "taken as the last
  terminal outcome", "will it pass") and softens timing ("often six to eighteen
  months").
- **Say what it is.** "The NL Gov Data tool", "research methods" rather than
  "products", "point of entry", and the practitioner's own terms ("stakeholder
  mapping", "political landscape", "internetconsultatie").
- **Frame examples as examples.** She adds a line that the examples are a
  sample and "the possibilities, however, reach further".
- **Complete lists over summaries.** She replaced a one-sentence summary of the
  sources with the full grouped list of all 18.
- **Price stated, plainly.** Two options, each with a name, a monthly price "excl.
  VAT", "first month free", one paragraph and four bullets; no "we propose it once
  we know your dossiers".
- **Dutch documents use *u*** throughout (unlike her prospect email, which uses
  *je*). Her Dutch is natural rather than translated: "uitzoeken", "de stand van
  zaken", "skills op maat", "zelf in gebruik", "legt uw vragen bij ons neer".
- **Format:** she edits in Word and sends a client the PDF. Two typos to catch in
  her Dutch: "stand van zake" (zaken) and "Één" at the start of a sentence (Eén).
